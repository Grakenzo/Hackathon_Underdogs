# Impact measurement

## The problem we're addressing

The MentorME brief names "understanding local recruitment processes" as a barrier for international talent. Our specific claim: people self-reject from jobs they are eligible for because they cannot read the ad.

The headline finding from our data supports this: not one of ten Brisbane IT support ads required a completed qualification. Every one said "or equivalent experience." That finding is invisible to someone reading a single ad.

## How we would measure success

### Primary proxy indicators

These are measurable without follow-up data from employers.

| Indicator | How to measure | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Quiz completion rate | % of users who reach the suggestions screen | Not yet collected |
| Detail screen engagement | % of users who open a role and switch tabs | Not yet collected |
| Time on "The words" tab | Average seconds spent reading jargon explanations | Not yet collected |
| Checklist completion | % of "Get ready" items ticked before session ends | Not yet collected |

### Secondary outcome indicators

These require follow-up and are harder to collect, but are the real measures of impact.

| Indicator | How to measure |
|---|---|
| Applications completed | Self-reported: "Did you apply for a role after using Pathfinder?" |
| Self-reported confidence | Pre/post survey: "How confident are you in understanding what this job requires?" (1–5) |
| Time to first application draft | Self-reported: time from finding an ad to starting a cover letter |

### The measure we are most confident in

Self-reported confidence before and after using the tool. It is quick to collect, directly tied to our stated problem, and does not require access to employer data.

## Who would implement this

A university careers service or a migrant employment service. They already have relationships with the target user group and the infrastructure to run a follow-up survey. The tool itself needs no integration — it runs as a web app with no login required, no data stored.

## What success looks like at scale

A meaningful reduction in self-rejection among international students applying for entry-level IT roles in Brisbane. Specifically: more students applying for roles they previously ruled themselves out of because they misread "desirable" as "required."
